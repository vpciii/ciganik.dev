## Services

### AI-assisted development, done carefully
AI coding tools can make a team dramatically faster, or quietly fill a
codebase with plausible-looking mistakes. Which one you get depends on
process. I use Claude Code and Cursor every day inside a spec-first
workflow: written specs and decisions up front, tests that trace back to
requirements, and human review of every change. I help teams adopt the
tools and that discipline together.

**Good fit if:** your developers are experimenting with AI tools and you
want consistency, or you've tried them and the results were uneven.

### AI-powered features & agents
Adding an LLM to a product is easy to demo and hard to make dependable.
I design and build AI features, multi-step agents, and Model Context
Protocol (MCP) servers that connect AI tools to your systems. I build
them with clear boundaries on what the AI can touch, tests that check
behavior, and an honest look at where a model shouldn't be in the loop
at all.

**Good fit if:** you have a use case in mind and want it built properly,
or you need to know whether AI is the right tool for it.

**See it in practice:**
- [opn-mcp](https://github.com/vpciii/opn-mcp) is a read-only MCP
  server that lets AI assistants monitor a firewall's security health
  without being able to change anything.
- [agent-framework](https://github.com/vpciii/agent-framework) is a
  work-in-progress orchestration layer where AI agents plan, build, and
  check each other's work, with a human signing off on every change.

### Architecture & modernization
Plenty of important software is ten or twenty years old and still
running. I've spent a career bridging old systems and new ones, from
lab instruments to Java/Spring applications. I modernize in steps you
can reverse, keeping the current system running until its replacement
has proven itself. AI tools now make that kind of migration faster to
plan and safer to test. For new systems, I help you make the
architectural decisions that are expensive to reverse, and write them
down.

**Good fit if:** you have a critical application only one person
understands, or you're about to make a big technical bet.

### High-stakes & regulated systems
Nearly thirty years of healthcare software taught me how to build
systems that handle sensitive data: HIPAA-compliant architecture,
security reviews and remediation, and HL7 / Mirth Connect integration
with hospital EMRs. The same habits carry over to any domain where
privacy, auditability, or safety matter.

*[Say on the page: I'm an engineer, not an auditor or lawyer. I make
systems defensible; formal compliance attestation is a separate

**Good fit if:** your system touches health, financial, or personal
data, or it has to integrate with healthcare systems.

### Fractional technology leadership
Small organizations and nonprofits often need senior technical judgment
but can't justify a full-time CTO. I've served as a nonprofit's CIO for
over a decade, handling its technology strategy, vendor and build-vs-buy
decisions, infrastructure, and budget. I also mentor University of
Pittsburgh capstone teams.

**Good fit if:** you're making technology decisions, including whether
and how to use AI, without a senior technologist in the room.

## About

I'm an electrical engineer by training (BSEE, University of Pittsburgh)
and I've been writing software since 1990. I've worked across nearly
every layer of the stack, and AI is the biggest change to how software
gets built that I've seen.

I started in embedded systems at AMETEK: assembly-language firmware,
custom circuit boards, and real-time signal processing for analytical
instruments. I was also putting instrument control on the web well
before that was common. As a contract engineer at McNair Software I
wrote firmware for FDA-regulated home dialysis equipment, and I built a
lab automation system that cut manual testing time by 60%.

From there, healthcare became the through-line. I built pharmacy
informatics tools at Keystone Therapeutics, including clinical decision
support, antimicrobial stewardship, and training simulators. One of
those products, Doseresponse, was acquired by MedKeeper / Becton
Dickinson. I then spent fifteen years there as a Staff Software
Engineer, building HIPAA-compliant systems used by hospital networks and
major health insurers.

For more than a decade I've also volunteered as CIO of a nonprofit that
connects new mothers with experienced mentors by text message. I built
its secure messaging platform and I'm now leading its modernization.

Today I work independently. My focus is helping teams adopt AI
development tools and build AI into their products, using the same
care about correctness that regulated software demands. I write my
process down and publish it, because I think the discipline matters more
than the tools.

I'm based in Western Pennsylvania, north of Pittsburgh, and work with
clients anywhere, remotely.
